**Not on day one:** quote desks (heavy integration), warehouse cameras (licensing), healthcare (HIPAA paperwork), IT firms (they compete), shoplifting and restaurant tables (lowest edge scores).

**How to go about it**

1. Clear the basics: check the employment contract allows side work, fill in the exclusion list, form an LLC, get liability insurance, and have a lawyer write the pilot agreement and the call-recording and AI notices.
2. Build the intake agent once, on a demo number prospects can call; configure it per client rather than building per client.
3. Use the 10 verified audits as leave-behinds: walk into the shops, email the firms. The founder does all outreach.
4. Sell a 2–4 week paid pilot on after-hours calls with an agreed success measure, then convert to setup plus a monthly care fee. Keep 2–3 pilots at a time.
5. After the first win: ask for a reference, add the certificate desk, and run a physical-business batch for sensor leads.

**Biggest risk:** ready-made AI receptionists already exist. The pitch has to rest on wiring the agent into the client's own booking and work-order systems and looking after it monthly, not on answering calls.

## First-customer plan: three tracks

### What the advice research says

Before finalising the plan, seven DeepSeek researchers studied seven questions: how small AI agencies won their first clients, how small businesses buy AI, packaging and keeping clients, help for New Jersey founders, legal and insurance risk, where each target trade meets vendors, and partner programs. Six of the seven briefs passed the second check. The legal and insurance brief has 4 open notes about wording, which are labelled. Claude re-opened nine of the key sources and all nine matched: the LLC fees, NJSBDC Middlesex, the NJSBA table price, Big I NJ dues, the Retell affiliate terms, the tech E&O price, MyCase commissions, NJAA vendor access and the NJ invention law.

**What changes the plan**

1. **Referrals and proof, not cold volume.**
   - 71% of professional-services buyers find a firm by asking someone ([Hinge](https://hingemarketing.com/library/article/new_study_highlights_how_buyers_buy_professional_services), 822 buyers, older study).
   - Cold-email replies averaged 0.45% across 7.5 million emails in 2025 ([Belkins via The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/b2b-outreach-tragedy-of-the-commons); this is vendor data).
   - One founder needed 487 LinkedIn requests to get 8 calls, 4 pilots and 2 paying clients ([Dietrich](https://www.fabiandietrich.com/blog/first-year-in-sales.html); a single account, so weak evidence).
2. **Publish prices.**
   - 69% of 511 B2B buyers say unclear pricing is their top frustration with vendors ([Mixology 2025](https://mixology-digital.com/hubfs/PDF%20downloads/B2B%20Research%20Report_2025_Final_Linked.pdf)).
   - 46% of small-business buyers want prices on the vendor's site ([SMB Group 2024](https://www.smb-gr.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/2024-SMB-Buying-Journey-Final.pdf)).
   - Over 80% of buyers look at a firm's website before contacting it, even when referred (Hinge).
3. **Most prospects don't use AI yet, so qualify on relevance.**
   - About 18–20% of US firms use AI, and the most common reason for not using it is "it doesn't apply to us" ([Census 2026](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2026/05/ai-use-businesses.html)).
   - Use rises with size: 21% of firms with 1–9 staff, 48% of firms with 50+ staff ([NFIB 2025](https://www.nfib.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2025-NFIB-Technology-Survey.pdf)).
   - In the NJ/PA/DE Fed district, 37% of small firms use AI and 57% of those are still experimenting ([Philadelphia Fed](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/community-development/credit-and-capital/how-is-ai-impacting-small-business-performance-evidence-from-the-third-district)).
4. **The owner decides, mostly on cost and ease of use.**
   - 69% of small-business tech buyers decide alone.
   - Their top shortlist reasons are cost-effectiveness (43%), integration (34%) and ease of use (33%) (SMB Group).
   - 80% sped up their AI plans because competitors were using it ([US Chamber 2025](https://www.uschamber.com/assets/documents/Empowering-Small-Business-Report-2025.pdf)).
5. **Don't promise savings.** Over 70% of AI-using firms saw no change in labour costs or sales; the gains were in productivity (Philadelphia Fed).
6. **Pilot and payment terms.**
   - A paid pilot needs a price, a decision-maker, a success measure and an end date ([Purdue](https://www.buildpurdue.org/blog/charge-startup-pilot)).
   - Only 16% of agencies bill fully upfront, and 71% say at least one invoice in four is paid late ([Ignition, 273 agencies](https://www.ignitionapp.com/news/2025-agency-pricing-cashflow-report)).
   - 57% of agencies lose $1,000–5,000 a month to unbilled extra work.
7. **Keeping clients takes a routine.**
   - 68% of 778 agencies lost clients in 2024 ([Agency Core](https://www.agencycore.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Agency-Core-Executive-Summary-Report.pdf)).
   - Formal onboarding, a named owner, agreed measures and regular reviews go with longer client relationships ([Promethean](https://prometheanresearch.com/client-retention-rate/)).
8. **Risk.**
   - Tech errors-and-omissions insurance averages $110 a month for IT businesses, and it is claims-made ([Insureon](https://www.insureon.com/technology-business-insurance/errors-omissions)).
   - Most policies are silent on AI, and some carriers now exclude it ([Aon 2026](https://assets.aon.com/-/media/files/aon/reports/2026/aon-ai-fact-sheet-2026.pdf)).
   - AI voices count as "artificial voice" under the federal robocall law ([FCC 24-17](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-17A4.pdf)), so outbound calls need consent.
   - NJ law protects inventions made on your own time and equipment, except ones related to the employer's business ([N.J.S.A. 34:1B-265](https://pub.njleg.state.nj.us/Bills/2016/PL17/346_.PDF)).
   - NJ lawyers must verify AI output and protect client confidentiality ([NJ Supreme Court, 2024](https://www.njcourts.gov/sites/default/files/notices/2024/01/n240125a.pdf)), so law firms will check us against those rules.
9. **Partner programs pay little early on.**
   - Retell, Vapi and Telnyx publish no reseller commission. Retell's affiliate program pays 15% for 12 months ([Retell](https://www.retellai.com/affiliate)).
   - Clio's Silver tier needs 100 active Clio customer accounts, and Intuit's partner tiers cost $300–4,500 a month.
   - MyCase pays IT and bookkeeping consultants 10% of a new client's annual contract value for referrals ([MyCase](https://www.mycase.com/partners/consultant-program/)).
   - Among 147 programs that publish a rate, the median referral fee is 20% ([Promethean](https://prometheanresearch.com/partner-program-commission-rates/)).
10. **Don't plan on state money.** The NJEDA micro-loan is closed, and both the Improvement Grant and the Small Business Fund exclude home-based businesses ([NJEDA](https://www.njeda.gov/smallbusinessfund/)).

**How to run it**

1. **Weeks 0–2 (to late November), groundwork:**
   - Read the employment contract's invention, confidentiality, non-solicit and outside-work clauses. Keep all work on personal devices and personal time.
   - Form the LLC ($100) and open a business bank account.
   - Get tech E&O and cyber insurance quotes, and ask the broker in writing whether AI errors are covered, silent or excluded.
   - File the NJSBDC eRFC request form and ask for a SCORE mentor.
   - Have a lawyer write one pilot agreement:
     - liability capped at the fees paid, with no indirect damages;
     - a data-handling addendum;
     - the client reviews the agent's outputs;
     - inbound calls only.
2. **Weeks 1–3, credibility:**
   - Build a one-page site showing the price card.
   - Write a one-page data-handling sheet: where data lives, no training on client data, and who can see it.
   - Record the three demos and label each one "demonstration, not a client result".
   - For law firms, add a note on how the agent fits the NJ Supreme Court's AI guidelines.
3. **Weeks 2–9, finding buyers, per track:**
   - Recruit 3–5 referral partners who already sell to these businesses. Pay about 15% of first-year collected revenue, with a 90-day protection window.
   - Contact 5–10 named businesses a week, each message citing what its audit found.
   - Follow up at most twice, each time with something useful.
   - Answer every inquiry the same day, and ask every contact how they heard of us.
4. **The pitch:**
   - Lead with one task and a before-and-after count, framed as keeping up with competitors and focused on cost and ease of use.
   - Don't promise headcount or revenue savings.
5. **Pilot terms:**
   - Paid, with a named owner, one success measure and an end date.
   - 50% deposit at signing.
   - Exclusions written down, with a change-order rule for anything extra.
   - A 30-day window to convert to setup and care.
   - The care fee has a 3-month minimum and includes a monthly 30-minute review.
6. **Measure and decide:**
   - Count referral partners, contacts, conversations and paid pilots per track every week.
   - At the end of January, keep the one or two tracks with paid pilots and pause the rest.
   - In February, take the NJSBA table if track A is alive.
7. **Capacity:** one custom build at a time. Track C is buy-and-configure, so it can run alongside a track A or B build.
8. **Design partners:** the first two clients pay for the pilot, get 50% off setup, and agree to an anonymised write-up and a reference. Ask for referrals at every pilot close.

**Why these three:** each has different buyers and a different risk. Track A competes with cheap ready-made tools, B depends on access to the agency's system, and C depends on hardware and walk-ins. Each also has a next use: A's intake core serves all six intake clients, B's document pipeline fits the CPA close and federal QC jobs later, and C's sensor hub fits leak sensors for property managers.

- Insurance at roughly $110–240 a month, until real quotes arrive.
- Referral fees are paid only out of collected revenue.

**Not doing yet:**

## Zero to one: action plan from the audits

Written 2026-11-14 from the 25 opportunity audits (15 passed independent checks) and the 26 website health checks (16 passed), on top of the first-customer plan dated 2026-11-12. "Zero to one" here means: first paying customer, then first monthly care fee. This is Claude's judgment from the audits; the response rates are unknown until the first ten conversations.

### What the audits change

1. **The front door comes before the agent, for everyone.** All 25 deep audits put a front-door repair first (a broken or unreadable website, an unclaimed Google profile, call links that fail, no quote or booking path), and the 26 health checks are businesses whose website is hacked, parked or gone. The agent is opportunity two in every audit. So the opener is not "do you want an AI receptionist" but "here is what your customers see today, dated and screenshotted".
2. **For the smallest shops, the agent alone does not pay for itself on time saved.** The modelled yearly value of an intake agent is $3,000–10,000 for most of the solo shops (a salon, a printer, a mechanic, a sole-practitioner lawyer), which is about the $5,400–7,800 a year the care fee costs. Where it clearly pencils: Artheon's office-engineer and pursuit agents ($45,000–95,000 and $30,000–65,000 a year), Distinct Engineering's bids and dispatch ($15,000–45,000 and $8,000–20,000), Lozito Law's closing coordination ($9,000–35,000), Federation Logistics' quote intake ($7,000–23,000), Midlantic's work orders and delinquencies ($5,000–17,000), Deborah Rose's bilingual intake ($9,000–23,000). All of these are modelled staff hours on stated assumptions, not cash, and must not be quoted as promises.
3. **Nine of the 25 have no named decision-maker anywhere public** (Yuhas, Kelly & Company, Hollander, Rakoty, Adway, Amertech, Distinct Engineering, the auto shop, B. Nathaniel). These need a visit or a phone call to find the owner; an email to nobody will not work.
4. **Some are off-limits or limited.** Molnar Roofing (a Chapter 7 filing by a company of that name, unconfirmed) and Ajulia Executive Search (a state stop-work order): do not contact until resolved. Bussel Realty: fine to contact, but only about the 404 contact page and missing enquiry form; the "hack" was our own browser's tab marker. Healthcare (First Rehab, Crystal Smiles, Rickards, Girgis, Hua Kang): a website fix handles no patient data and is fine; an intake agent is not, until a BAA is affordable. Artheon has 720 staff, well over the 200-staff cap used so far; keep it as one deliberate bigger bet or drop it.
5. **Timing matters for three of them.** Premier Properties' leasing waves are May–June and August–September, so a pilot has to start by March. Rakoty's audit and Form 990 season runs January to May, so December or January is the time to talk about document chasing. Roofers and paving contractors fix websites in winter.

## Legal and licensing limits

New Jersey treats CCTV, and AI used for physical security, as a licensed alarm business, so sensor work can start now but security camera work needs a licence or a licensed partner. Claude read this from the statute itself; it is a reading, not legal advice.

- **The rule:** N.J.S.A. 45:5A-2 as amended by [P.L.2021, c.2](https://pub.njleg.gov/bills/2020/PL21/2_.PDF) (approved January 21, 2021) covers selling, installing, servicing or maintaining electronic security systems, including CCTV and access control, and "artificial intelligence and evolving technology used for physical security applications".
- **Installation includes the site survey and system design,** not just mounting hardware.
- **Exempt:** licensed electrical contractors and their employees, and regulated phone and cable companies ([P.L.1997, c.305](https://pub.njleg.gov/bills/9697/PL97/305_.HTM)).

| Work | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Temperature, leak, vibration sensors | Appears outside: not security systems |
| Operations cameras (dock flow, table state, counting, quality checks) | Grey area: get a written answer from the Division of Consumer Affairs first |
| Shoplifting detection, intrusion, gate monitoring | Licensed work |

**Rules for every install:** no face recognition or biometric ID, audio off, a person reviews every alert, no automated accusations, written staff notice and a written retention schedule. A lawyer writes the notices; the consultancy does not. The FTC [banned Rite Aid](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/12/rite-aid-banned-using-ai-facial-recognition-after-ftc-says-retailer-deployed-technology-without) from facial recognition for five years after false matches.

**Software licence:** Ultralytics YOLO detectors are AGPL-3.0, so a commercial product needs their enterprise licence or an Apache-licensed detector such as RF-DETR.

**Law-firm intake:** the agent must never give legal advice; the firm's own lawyer should approve its script.

## How the research pipeline works

DeepSeek v4.1 Flash agents do the research and writing, a second DeepSeek agent re-opens the sources and gives PASS or FAIL, and failures are revised until they pass. Claude writes the briefs, checks between phases and reads anything the agents could not open.

1. **Map:** find candidate businesses per industry within 15 miles of Milltown.
2. **Research:** one agent per business gathers public, sourced facts, now including physical operations (kitchens, coolers, docks, fleet).
3. **Analyse:** three opportunities per business with a lead score out of 100; with camera and sensor ideas preferred, at least one must be a camera or sensor idea with a walk-in checklist.
4. **Write:** one-page audits for the top 10.
5. **Verify and revise:** repeat until PASS.

**Lessons about running the agents**

- Every first draft fails verification: 11 of 11 audits and 16 of 16 research briefs. Plan on 2–5 rounds; once most pass, revise only the failures.
- Common catches: prices not on the cited page, figures pinned to the wrong model or year, preprints called peer-reviewed, vendor claims rated too strongly, arithmetic that does not follow from its own assumptions, and "businesses like yours" lines with no source.
- Put the writing rules into the first brief: shown arithmetic, no peer generalisations, no claims about the consultancy's own clients.
- Headcounts from the mapper are guesses; confirm size from the business's own pages (one 3PL listed at 51–200 had 300+ staff).
- Public evidence is biased toward phone and paperwork problems; camera and sensor needs are only visible on a walk-in.
- Agents can't parse some government PDFs; Claude reads those directly.
- Practical: close the agent's input stream or it hangs; run 5–6 agents at a time on the home server.
