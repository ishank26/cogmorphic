# AI Consultancy: Prospect Review and Outreach Plan

Nov 24, 2026 · @Antoinette

## Bottom line

The research is careful enough to start outreach with the verified businesses, but not with all of them, and not without a same-day recheck.

- **Almost every business has the same problem.** Their "front door" is broken: a website that is hacked, parked or down, a Google profile nobody claimed, call buttons that dial wrong numbers, no way to ask for a quote, and nothing that answers after hours. AI is the second step in every audit, never the first.
- **The analysis is mostly solid.** Every claim carries a link and a date (13 and 14 November), an independent checker reopened the sources, and savings estimates show their maths. About two thirds passed that check: 15 of 25 deep audits, 16 of 26 website checks, and all 10 audits from the 11 November run.
- **The failures matter for outreach.** Several unverified audits contain mistakes an owner would spot at once, like saying a shop publishes no prices when it does, or greeting someone by a guessed first name. Use only the passed ones until the rest are fixed.
- **Approach in person, with the free website check, not with "AI".** Start with five verified, nearby businesses with a named owner. Recheck each site the morning you go, and do the repo's own setup steps (day-job contract, LLC, insurance, agreement) before any paid work.

Everything below about a business is the research's own claim, as observed on 13 November 2026 unless noted. I couldn't reopen the websites from here, so none of it is rechecked.

## Is the analysis good enough?

Yes for choosing whom to talk to and what to show them; no as a script to send unchanged. 42 of the 62 write-ups passed the independent check, and the 20 that didn't include errors an owner would notice in seconds.

### What's strong

- **Sourced and dated.** Every claim links to the page it came from, with the date it was seen, and each audit lists sources it dropped and why.
- **Independently checked.** A second agent reopened the sources and recomputed the maths. Failures are labelled, not hidden.
- **Honest about money.** Savings estimates show their arithmetic and are marked as assumptions, and the plan keeps them out of the pitch.
- **Catches real risks.** It flags a bankruptcy filing, a state stop-work order, New Jersey's licence rules for security cameras, and patient-data rules for the dental and medical offices.

### Where it falls short

| Problem | Example | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Errors in unverified audits | B. Nathaniel's audit says the site lists no prices; its shop page does. Hollander's cites a page that doesn't exist. Menlo's cites the wrong regulation | The owner knows their own business, so one wrong fact ends the conversation |
| Guessed names | "Hi Toby" (Hollander) comes from an email address; "Hi Steve" (Molnar) from a 2019 review | Greeting the wrong person reads as a mass mailing |
| Inference stated as fact | Yuhas's audit says after-hours calls go to voicemail; no source shows that | It's the exact claim the pilot is sold on |
| Plan and audits disagree | Lozito is in North Brunswick, not Milltown; Biondo's Google address is Monroe Township; Crystal Smiles is counted twice | Small, but it shows the plan wasn't reconciled |
| Already 11 days old | Advance Home's domain was pending deletion; Rickards' expires 6 December; Minieri's on 31 December | A site may be fixed or gone by the time you walk in |
| Screenshots not in the repo | They're kept locally (457 MB), and the checker couldn't find the ones promised for Molnar, Ajulia and Hometech | The walk-in pitch rests on a dated screenshot |
| Partial coverage | About 1,550 of 8,169 mapped businesses had been triaged when progress was last logged on 13 November | The "top 25" is the top of a sample, not the area |
| Rough scores | One analyst per business; the repo itself says gaps under 3 points are noise | Don't over-read the ranking |
| No named owner for 9 of 25 | Yuhas, Kelly & Company, Hollander, Rakoty, Adway, Amertech, Distinct, the auto shop, B. Nathaniel | These need a visit or a call to find who decides |

The research can't tell you whether owners will pay. Its own advice section found cold email reply rates around 0.45% in 2025, and AI use under 20% among the smallest firms, so expect the first clients to come from in-person visits and referrals.

## How to approach them

Lead with a free, dated one-page check of what their customers see, offer a fixed-price fix, and bring up AI only once the fix is delivered. That matches the research's own conclusion: every audit puts the front door first, and "AI" alone is a hard sell to a small shop.

### How to make contact

- **In person for local shops and trades; email then a phone call for law firms and the bigger firms.** Keep it to 5 to 10 new contacts a week and at most two follow-ups.
- **Say what you saw, not what you concluded.** "When I tapped the website link on your Google listing this morning, it opened a gambling page" is safe. "Your site was hacked" may not be.
- **Leave the printout with them whether or not they buy.** It costs you nothing, builds goodwill, and is how referrals start.
- **Keep savings figures out of the pitch.** The audits' dollar ranges are modelled guesses, and your own price card promises none.
- **Leave sensitive public records out.** Hughes's audit cites a 2018 court list of attorneys who missed annual filings, and its email hints at it. Don't raise it.
- **Mention you're local.** You live in the area these businesses serve, which no national AI-receptionist company can say.

### First messages

Walk-in, for a broken website:

> Hi, I'm Antoinette, I run a small tech consultancy here in Milltown. When I opened the website address for your office this morning, it went to a gambling page instead of your business. I printed what I saw so you have it. The domain is still yours, so it's usually fixable. If you'd like, I can fix it for a fixed price, usually within a week or two.

Email, for a law firm:

> Subject: The CALL NOW button on your website
>
> Hi Mark,
>
> I'm a Milltown-based consultant who helps small firms with their websites and after-hours calls. On \[date\] I noticed the CALL NOW button on your site doesn't do anything when tapped on a phone, so a new client can't call you from it.
>
> I'd be glad to show you what I found in 20 minutes, and how a short paid pilot could answer after-hours calls and text you a summary.
>
> Antoinette

The repo says the $900 to $1,800 fix price is still an estimate. I'd price the first two or three jobs at the low end in return for a Google review and permission to use them as a case study, since buyers in the research trusted case studies above everything else.

## Fix before reaching out

These come from the repo's own plan, which set them for 21 and 28 November; I can't tell from the repo which are done.

- [ ] Read your day-job contract's outside-work, invention and non-solicit clauses. The plan puts this before everything else.
- [ ] Form the LLC ($100), open a business account, and pick a name and domain.
- [ ] Put up a one-page site with the price card, and use a business email address. Prospects will look you up.
- [ ] Bind tech E&O and cyber insurance before any paid job (the research found about $110 and $129 a month).
- [ ] Have a lawyer review the pilot agreement and a short fixed-price work order.
- [ ] Remove the "Owner notes, delete before printing" block from the price card, and the dollar figures from leave-behinds.
- [ ] Fix or drop the 20 unverified write-ups, and correct the plan's mismatches (Lozito's and Biondo's towns, Crystal Smiles counted twice).
- [ ] Recheck each website and retake dated screenshots the morning you visit.
- [ ] Keep one sheet: who, what you showed, their objection, next step, and how they heard of you.
