## 3. How to run the meeting

The yes comes when they watch their own messy message become a clean draft, and the draft catches something a tired person would miss. Talk less than they do.

**Before you go:** open that lead's demo on your laptop, signed in to Claude, and draft one sample message to confirm live mode works. Live drafting only runs while you are signed in. Don't email the demo link; it is private.

1. **Ask (3 min).** "What does a busy morning in the office look like?" Then: "Can you pull up a request from this week?" Let them pick it.
2. **Scrub (2 min).** "Take out names and account numbers first." If they won't paste, use the sample on the page.
3. **Paste and draft (6 min).** Point at the catch it flags (the missing appointment, the wrong revision) and the gap it marks "Not stated." Then stop talking.
4. **Their numbers (4 min).** Hand them the keyboard for the hours calculator. Read the result out loud as hours a month, and dollars at their own hourly cost.
5. **The ask (5 min).** "Let's try this one job for two weeks. Your \[CSR / dispatcher\] approves every draft, and we measure the time before and during." Then the price (section 4) and a start date.
6. **Before you leave.** Write down the start date, who approves, which mailbox gets the copies, and the one number you will measure.

Say out loud: the assistant reads and drafts only, it never sends, and a person sends from their own mailbox. Quote no savings figures of your own.

## 4. The close

Ask for a paid two-week pilot on one job, with a start date, before you leave the room. Pick your price below before your first meeting and say it the same way every time.

**The pilot, in one breath:** one job, two weeks, their person approves every draft, copies of the relevant emails go to one mailbox they own, and you measure minutes per item before and during. It ends on a set date with a results meeting.

### Pick a pilot price

| Option | Price | Best for | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Founding pilot | $500, paid at signing, credited to setup | Your first two clients (NXT, JVN, or the first trades shop) | Below your price card. Ask for a case study and one referral in return |
| B. Standard pilot | $1,200, half at signing, credited to setup | Trades shops and agencies after your first case study | The low end of your price card's $1,200 to $1,500 |
| C. Back-office pilot, paid upfront | $1,500 to $2,500, paid before day 1 | Property managers, law, wealth, and any risky client such as Midlantic | Fewer yeses, but no chasing invoices |

I'd start with A for the first two, then B. Avoid fully free pilots: a client who paid nothing has no reason to send you their real messages.

These figures come from your price card, which calls itself an estimate. They are yours to change.

## 5. After the pilot: turn it into a client

Book the results meeting on day 1, for day 14. Show the numbers, then offer the next step from the demo's "When it works, next" list.

1. **Day 1: set the baseline.** Time 10 items the old way.
2. **Show one page on day 14:** items handled, minutes per item before and during, edits needed, and dollars a month at their hourly cost.
3. **Offer one price, not a range,** on a one-page proposal: setup minus the pilot credit, the monthly fee, and a start date the day after the pilot ends. Your card puts setup at $2,500 to $3,500 and the monthly fee at $450 to $650, three-month minimum.
4. **Add the second job** from the demo's "next" list, priced as a small add-on, so the monthly plan grows.
5. **Ask for three things,** win or lose: a Google review, a short case study you can show other shops in the same trade, and one name they'd introduce you to.
6. **No answer by day 14?** Drafting pauses on day 15. Follow up once on day 17.
7. **If the numbers are weak,** say so, fix one thing, and offer one more week at no charge. Don't stretch a pilot past three weeks.

One case study from a local HVAC shop makes the next four HVAC meetings easier. Use it first with the shops in the same trade.

### Where the advice conflicts

- **Free work.** Some say never work free; others took a friend at a poor margin for a reference ([HN 47822940](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47822940)). A cheap paid pilot (option A) splits the difference.
- **Free vs paid pilots.** Paid pilots weed out buyers who aren't serious; free ones lower the buyer's risk ([HN 18259804](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=18259804), about larger companies).
- **Cold outreach.** People selling outreach templates say it scales; owners and practitioners say phone and in-person win in traditional trades.
- **Content.** One author got inbound leads from writing; another got almost nothing in seven years ([HN 30237149](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=30237149)).

No one reported showing a live demo on the prospect's own message, so that part of your plan is untested by others. It fits the advice to lead with their problem, in their words.
